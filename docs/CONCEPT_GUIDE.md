# FINTRAXIS Concept Guide

### 1. How to read FINTRAXIS (start here)

FINTRAXIS is a **screening and learning tool** for credit analysis. It reads a company's published financial statements, calculates standard ratios, and shows warning signals. It is **not** a credit rating and **not** investment advice.

Every number in the app has three things you should check: **what it is** (definition), **where it came from** (source), and **when it is from** (period). Look for the source badge next to a number.

**Source badges**
- **Disclosed in filing:** copied from the company's annual report or regulatory filing (10-K in the US, annual report or BSE/NSE filing in India).
- **Calculated:** computed by the app from disclosed figures using the formula shown.
- **Estimated:** the app made an assumption. The assumption is stated.
- **Simulated:** the result of a what-if scenario you ran. Not a forecast.
- **Demonstration/seed data:** sample data stored for demonstration. Do not treat it as current.

**N/D** means "Not Disclosed / not available". It is not zero. A zero means the company reported zero. N/D means the app does not have the input, so it refuses to guess.

**As-of dates.** Financial statements are from a fiscal period end (for example 31-Mar-2025). Market data (price, market cap) has its own timestamp and may be delayed. Never read a filing figure as "today's" figure.

**Currency and scale.** Indian companies are shown in ₹ crores or lakhs (1 crore = 10 million, 1 lakh = 100,000) with Indian digit grouping (12,34,567). US companies are shown in US$ millions with standard grouping (1,234,567). Do not compare a ₹ figure to a $ figure without a conversion note.

**Negative numbers** appear in brackets, e.g. (1,250) means minus 1,250. **x** after a number means "times" (3.5x = 3.5 times). **bps** means basis points: 100 bps = 1%.

### 2. The three financial statements

- **Balance Sheet:** a snapshot on one date. Assets (what the company owns) = Liabilities (what it owes) + Equity (owners' share). The app checks that this equality holds. If it does not, the data has a problem.
- **Income Statement (Profit & Loss):** what happened over a period: revenue minus costs gives profit.
- **Cash Flow Statement:** actual cash moving in and out, split into operating, investing and financing activities.

### 3. Key line items

- **Revenue / Sales:** money earned from selling goods or services.
- **EBIT:** earnings before interest and tax. Profit from operations.
- **EBITDA:** EBIT + depreciation + amortization. A rough measure of operating cash earnings. The Methodology page states whether the app uses the reported figure or EBIT + D&A.
- **PAT (Profit After Tax) / Net Income:** the final profit.
- **CFO (Cash Flow from Operations):** cash generated by the core business.
- **Capex:** money spent on buying or upgrading long-term assets.
- **Total debt:** short-term borrowings + current portion of long-term debt + long-term borrowings. The Methodology page states how lease liabilities are treated.
- **Cash & equivalents:** cash and very liquid short-term holdings.
- **Equity / Net worth:** assets minus liabilities.
- **Tangible Net Worth (TNW):** net worth minus intangible assets (goodwill, patents, software).

### 4. Ratios (formula, plain meaning, what it does NOT tell you)

| Ratio | Formula | Plain meaning | Does NOT tell you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net Debt | Total debt − cash & equivalents | Debt left after using cash. Negative = more cash than debt | Which debt is due soon |
| Net Debt / EBITDA | Net Debt ÷ EBITDA | Years of operating earnings needed to repay net debt. Lower is safer | Works poorly when EBITDA is tiny or negative |
| Interest Coverage | EBIT ÷ Interest expense | How many times earnings cover interest. Higher is safer. Below about 1.5x is a warning | Whether principal can be repaid |
| Current Ratio | Current assets ÷ Current liabilities | Can the company pay bills due within a year? | Quality of the assets |
| Quick Ratio | (Current assets − Inventory) ÷ Current liabilities | Stricter version without inventory | Timing of payments |
| Cash Ratio | Cash ÷ Current liabilities | Strictest liquidity test | Access to credit lines |
| Free Cash Flow (FCF) | CFO − Capex | Cash left after keeping the business running | One-off items |
| Gross / Operating / Net Margin | Profit measure ÷ Revenue | How much of each rupee/dollar of sales becomes profit | Compare only within the same industry |
| ROE | Net income ÷ Equity | Return earned on owners' money | Can be inflated by heavy debt |
| ROA | Net income ÷ Total assets | Return on everything owned | Industry differences |
| Debt / Equity | Total debt ÷ Equity | Borrowed money vs owners' money | Cost or maturity of debt |

### 5. Indian appraisal metrics

- **TOL / TNW:** Total Outside Liabilities ÷ Tangible Net Worth. TOL = all liabilities owed to outsiders (borrowings, creditors, provisions). Indian bank appraisal uses it to see how much of the business is financed by outsiders vs the owners. A commonly used rule of thumb is about 3.00x, but the exact limit varies by bank and industry. The app's 3.00x is an **illustrative reference level you can change**, not a regulatory limit. Shows N/D if intangibles are missing.
- **Interest Service Coverage:** same idea as interest coverage, as used in Indian bank appraisal.
- **DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio):** (PAT + depreciation + interest) ÷ (interest + principal repayments due in the year). Banks look for roughly 1.25x or more, but limits vary. The app only shows DSCR when scheduled principal repayments are disclosed. Otherwise it shows a **clearly named proxy** (for example "Debt Service Proxy using current portion of long-term debt") and says it is not statutory DSCR.

### 6. Scores and screening models

**Altman Z''-Score** (a distress screen)
- Z'' = 6.56·X1 + 3.26·X2 + 6.72·X3 + 1.05·X4
- X1 = Working capital ÷ Total assets; X2 = Retained earnings ÷ Total assets; X3 = EBIT ÷ Total assets; X4 = Book equity ÷ Total liabilities.
- Common reading: above 2.60 = "safe zone", 1.10 to 2.60 = "grey zone", below 1.10 = "distress zone".
- Z'' is the variant meant for non-manufacturers and emerging markets. It is **not applicable to banks, NBFCs and other financial companies**.
- If any input (for example retained earnings) is missing, the app shows N/D. It does not estimate.
- It is a statistical screen from historical data, not a prediction that a company will fail.

**Beneish M-Score** (an earnings-quality screen)
- M = −4.84 + 0.920·DSRI + 0.528·GMI + 0.404·AQI + 0.892·SGI + 0.115·DEPI − 0.172·SGAI + 4.679·TATA − 0.327·LVGI
- It compares two consecutive years using eight indices (receivables, margins, asset quality, sales growth, depreciation, SG&A, accruals, leverage).
- Higher (less negative) values, commonly above about −1.78, suggest the accounts deserve a closer look.
- It is a **signal, not proof of manipulation**. Not applicable to financial companies. Needs two comparable years and every input, otherwise N/D.

**HHI (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index)** (concentration)
- HHI = sum of (each share in %)² for segments, customers or suppliers. Range 0 to 10,000.
- Example: one segment at 100% gives 10,000 (fully concentrated). Four equal segments of 25% each give 2,500.
- Threshold bands are shown with their source. Check also whether the disclosed segments add up to total revenue.

**Composite Health Score and tiers**
- A weighted combination of liquidity, leverage, coverage, profitability and cash flow indicators. The weights, cut-offs and each component's contribution are shown in the expandable breakdown.
- The tiers are **internal screening categories**. They are not credit ratings and do not match any rating agency's opinion.

### 7. Stress testing

- **Scenario Simulator:** a what-if tool. Press **Run Simulation** to see baseline vs stressed numbers side by side.
- **Floating-rate share:** the part of debt whose interest changes with market rates. If the company does not disclose it, the app uses the default you can see and change, and says it is an assumption.
- **Stressed interest** = Baseline interest + (Total debt × floating share × rate shock in bps ÷ 10,000).
  - Example: debt 1,000, floating share 50%, +200 bps: extra interest = 1,000 × 0.5 × 0.02 = 10.
- **Covenant (hurdle) headroom / Reverse stress:** instead of asking "what happens if EBITDA falls 20%?", it asks "how far can EBITDA fall before a limit is breached?"
  - For a leverage ceiling: breach EBITDA = Net Debt ÷ Ceiling. Headroom % = 1 − (Net Debt ÷ (Ceiling × EBITDA)).
  - Example: Net Debt 700, EBITDA 300 (2.33x), ceiling 3.50x. Breach EBITDA = 200. Headroom = 33.3% (EBITDA can fall by 100 before the ceiling is breached).
  - The hurdles are **user-defined screening thresholds, not the company's actual loan covenants**.

### 8. Data and trust features

- **Audit sidecar / source link:** tap or hover a figure to see currency, scale, fiscal period, filing form, filing date and the link.
- **Coverage panel:** a table showing which periods and statements exist and which line items are N/D. Gaps are shown on purpose.
- **Balance sheet identity check:** Assets must equal Liabilities + Equity within a small rounding tolerance.
- **Benchmarks:** an "Industry median" is only shown if it is backed by a cited dataset. Otherwise it is labeled "Illustrative reference level, user-adjustable".
- **Dependencies and concentration:** customers, suppliers and licenses are listed with the filing section that discloses them. Items from general public reporting are labeled that way.
- **Promoter pledging (India):** the share of promoters' holdings pledged as security for loans. High pledging is an early-warning sign. Shown only when taken from the shareholding pattern.

### 9. Reports and exports

- **IC (Investment Committee) memo:** a printable report with summary, findings, health assessment, vulnerabilities, dependencies, scenarios, sources and limitations. Footer shows page number, generation date/time and data sources.
- **CSV export:** exact figures and units.
- **Excel (.xlsx) export:** statements and ratios with a header block (company, currency, scale, period, source, time).
- **Print / PDF:** A4 layout. Check that exported figures match the screen.

### 10. Using the app

- **Login gate:** you must sign in first. **Quick Demo Entry** opens a demonstration session with restricted admin actions.
- **Idle timeout:** the session locks after a period of inactivity.
- **Command Palette (Ctrl + K):** jump to a company, module or action.
- **"?" key:** opens shortcuts and the Help & Glossary.
- **Demonstration environment notice:** on Vercel, changes you save may reset when the service restarts.

### 11. Limits you should know

- Based on published statements, so it is backward looking.
- Not a credit rating, not investment advice. Data may be delayed or incomplete.
- Ratios from different industries, currencies or fiscal calendars are not directly comparable.
- Financial companies (banks, NBFCs, insurers) need different models. Altman and Beneish are not applied to them.
